# Follow-up review for the phone app

Reviewed 20 October 2026, after the first usable interface and its initial browser tests. This is an additional focused check, not a new systematic review or another claimed 20-minute research session. The original uninterrupted research timing is retained separately in `original-timing.json`.

## Decisions retained

- Reward-based teaching, management, choice and gradual comfortable exposure remain the foundation. The [AVSAB 2025 board statement](https://avsab.org/resources/position-statements/) expressly reaffirms its 2021 position. No correction to that overall approach was needed.
- The [2024 counterconditioning review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168159124001539) was rechecked at the publisher’s abstract/highlights level. Its small and heterogeneous evidence base still does not establish one named game, exact distance, session length or success percentage as best for every dog. The app distinguishes classical trigger–reward pairings from rewarding an action.
- Noise recordings remain a separate controllable practice tool rather than proof of comfort with real storms/fireworks. Management and veterinary support for severe noise fear are retained. See the [noise-fear evidence review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/pmid/38067015/).
- Rechecking the [nosework study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0168159118304325) supports offering sniffing as enjoyable enrichment, not calling it a demonstrated anxiety cure.
- The core dog/visitor/knock/sound/alone-time and cooperative-care lessons were manually reviewed again. Retreat, barriers, voluntary starts, gradual changes, and medical/behavioral support boundaries were retained. A dog need not sit, become silent, or stare at the handler to receive food during classical counterconditioning.

## Corrections and clarifications made

1. Spreadsheet instructions were removed or rewritten for phone screens. ONLYOFFICE documentation records are not included in the phone app’s dog-training reference library.
2. The R05 source now opens the publisher’s article page directly and names its authors. Access is still honestly described as abstract/highlights review, not full-paper appraisal.
3. [Dogs Trust’s “reading” game](https://www.dogstrust.org.uk/dog-advice/training/tips-tricks/reading) was critically screened. Its spoken labels, gestures and box positions do not by themselves demonstrate reading. The app’s different optional picture-card lesson is explicitly visual cue learning, with no literacy claim.
4. [Object-label research in typical dogs](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-30201-1) and [rapid learning in exceptional dogs](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-021-81699-2) informed the optional toy-name experiment. Side swaps and neutral human cues are included; failure is not called disobedience or low intelligence. A single-item retrieve is explicitly insufficient evidence of name discrimination.
5. [VCA toothbrushing guidance](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/successful-toothbrushing-training-for-happy-dental-care) informed a small voluntary dental-preparation module. It calls for veterinary advice for pain and technique, pet-safe materials, very brief work and stopping at withdrawal. It gives no forced-jaw or drug instructions.
6. The new bow favors capturing a natural comfortable stretch. Physical positioning under the abdomen and forceful or prolonged stretches were not adopted. Backing up uses voluntary foot movement rather than crowding or a narrow chute.
7. Multiple stressful events are discussed without an invented universal cortisol/recovery clock. See [professional trigger-stacking guidance](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/training/how-to-avoid-trigger-stacking-in-dogs/). The dog’s individual comfortable baseline governs the next session.
8. Front-foot targets stay flat, hoops stay at floor level, and tunnels begin short, wide and secured. No unstable balance equipment, flips, high jumps, small ingestion-prone props, or strenuous stunt progression is included.
9. Prerequisites were checked for unnecessary gates. Enjoyable finding games do not require a perfected retrieve, a stationary weave needs a comfortable pass rather than a long between-legs hold, and a messenger toy does not require learning to carry a pouch first.

## Additional activities

Thirty-two original skills add 81 actual teaching steps. Examples include nose-ball rolling, [gentle bowling](https://www.dogstrust.org.uk/dog-advice/training/tips-tricks/dog-bowling), wide cone loops, a [slow stationary weave](https://www.akc.org/expert-advice/training/teach-dog-leg-weave/), safe prop retrieval and familiar-person delivery, food searches, voluntary scale practice and quiet targeting. These are instructional adaptations and enrichment ideas, not individually validated clinical protocols.

The new activities were added after the functional app and its first passing navigation/storage/backup checks. No numerical trick-count target was used. The purpose was variety while keeping the guides readable, component-based and testable.

## Evidence limits

The app is an educational guide, not an individual treatment assessment. No breed-specific anxiety treatment trial was identified in this focused work. Pit bulls and Staffies are treated as individual dogs whose body shape, strength, comfort, history and health matter. No dosing, diagnosis, universal threshold distance, guaranteed recovery time or promised treatment success is supplied.

Nine additional source records are saved in `additional-sources.json`; the app’s final dataset contains 67 dog-training/research/professional reference records. Original third-party papers are linked, not republished in the app.
