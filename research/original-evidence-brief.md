# Research brief: teaching useful skills and helping an anxious dog

Research date: 2026-10-20. This is a focused search with critical appraisal, not an exhaustive systematic review. Detailed citations and access limitations are in the four source-register JSON files. Teaching procedures in the eventual workbook are original adaptations rather than verbatim source protocols.

## The strongest practical starting point

Use rewards, protect the dog's ability to retreat, and arrange practice so the dog can succeed comfortably. The current [AVSAB position statement](https://avsab.org/resources/position-statements/) supports reward-based teaching and behavior modification (R01). This is a veterinary recommendation informed by effectiveness and welfare, rather than a claim that every reward-based exercise has been tested or that every study produces identical results.

[Vieira de Castro et al. (2020)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0225023) studied welfare differences between training schools, with important limits from nonrandom school selection (R03). [China et al. (2020)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/veterinary-science/articles/10.3389/fvets.2020.00508/full) found favorable response measures for reward-focused recall training, but trainer and seasonal differences complicate causal claims (R04).

The recommendation should not be oversold: [Johnson and Wynne (2024)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39335223/) found better suppression of lure chasing in their aversive condition than in the tested alternatives, although transfer to a novel arena was poor (R24). Stopping chase and treating fear are different research questions. That experiment does not establish that painful tools help a dog feel safer around visitors or noises.

## Changing feelings and teaching actions are different processes

The [2024 counterconditioning review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applanim.2024.106305) found generally favorable outcomes in a heterogeneous group of reports, with limitations from small samples and inconsistent methods (R05). This supports a careful framework, not a guaranteed result for a branded game. The publisher-indexed abstract was reviewed; attempts to retrieve a complete accessible copy were unsuccessful.

For emotional pairing, a manageable version of the trigger predicts food or enjoyable play. There is no need to ask for obedience first. In contrast, teaching a voluntary head turn, a hand target, or stationing rewards a particular action. [AVSAB's learning-theory explanation](https://avsab.org/should-you-use-a-secondary-reinforcer-clicker-in-counter-conditioning/) makes this distinction explicit (P29). The workbook separates those lesson types so the handler knows what is being changed.

## Noise fears

[Riemer's 2023 review](https://phaidra.vetmeduni.ac.at/api/object/o:2589/download) is a useful overview of training, management, medication evidence, and the limits of recordings (R06). The [2020 firework-treatment survey](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jveb.2020.04.005) reports encouraging caregiver experiences with reward pairing and relaxation, but self-reported improvement is not a randomized comparative effect (R07).

Use separate plans for controllable sound practice and unavoidable real events. A distant car-door click, a quiet knock, and actual fireworks may require different starting points. Calm response to a recording does not prove readiness for the real event. The dog may detect features that recordings omit.

Current [Dogs Trust fireworks guidance](https://www.dogstrust.org.uk/dog-advice/life-with-your-dog/seasonal/fireworks) supports safe hiding and calm reassurance when the dog seeks it (P27). Older sound-training documents sometimes discourage reassurance; that instruction is not adopted here. Human contact should remain the dog's choice.

## Other dogs and unfamiliar people

[VCA's people-and-animal fear guidance](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/fears-and-phobias-in-dogs-animals-and-people) explains medical assessment, distance, and careful exposure (P08). [Dogs Trust's reactivity overview](https://www.dogstrust.org.uk/dog-advice/training/unwanted-behaviours/reactive-dog) distinguishes fear from other possible motivations such as frustration (P04).

The goal can be comfortable coexistence without greeting. Do not lure the dog into a person's reach or set up close dog encounters to force habituation. An original lesson sequence can progress from a comfortable sighting, to positive pairing, to voluntary disengagement, and later to parallel movement or a wide pass. These stages are teaching adaptations; no large trial was identified here proving a named game is best for all reactive dogs.

## Knocking and visitors

[Yin et al. (2008)](https://cattledogpublishing.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Yinetal2008TT.pdf) directly examined a detailed reward-based arrival protocol in small laboratory and home experiments (R19). [Dogs Trust's visitor guide](https://www.dogstrust.org.uk/dog-advice/training/home/visitor-training) provides practical component ideas (P03).

The workbook separates: feeling better about the sound; learning a mat action; linking a knock to the mat; and practicing a person's arrival or movement. A dog that knows 'mat' has not therefore learned to feel safe with a stranger. A comfortable separate room remains a valid way to manage an ordinary visit.

## Relaxation and body language

[Battersea's mat guide](https://www.battersea.org.uk/pet-advice/dog-advice/my-dog-wont-settle-teaching-your-dog-be-calm-and-relaxed) provides a useful teaching progression (P02). Its unsupported biochemical claims are not needed. Treat mat targeting and voluntary relaxation as separate learning goals.

[Dogs Trust's body-language guide](https://www.dogstrust.org.uk/dog-advice/understanding-your-dog/body-language) supports observing the whole dog and the context (P06). No single sign tells the complete story. Quietness, eating, or a trained posture can coexist with distress. The workbook uses behavior descriptions and recovery observations rather than a purported diagnostic anxiety score.

## Medical contributions and professional help

[Lopes Fagundes et al. (2018)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/veterinary-science/articles/10.3389/fvets.2018.00017/full) links patterns of noise sensitivity and musculoskeletal pain in a small clinical sample (R10). A sudden change, touch sensitivity, late onset, or general deterioration warrants a veterinary conversation. This does not mean every anxious dog has pain.

Placebo-controlled trials support specific veterinary medicines for specific noise situations ([R15](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28213531/), [R16](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31568622/)). Those results justify discussing care with a veterinarian; they do not identify a prescription for this dog. The workbook contains no dosages. A [2026 CBD crossover study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jveb.2025.12.012) did not show benefit over placebo on its measured caregiver outcomes (R20); it does not support making supplements a required purchase.

## Pit bulls and Staffies

[Morrill et al. (2022)](https://www.zoology.ubc.ca/~otto/veg/Readings/morrill2022ancestry.pdf) reports large behavioral variation within breeds (R08). Genetics and breed tendencies can contribute, but breed alone does not tell us an individual's trigger distance, motivation, bite risk, or preferred reward. No pit bull/Staffy-specific anxiety intervention trial was identified in this focused search.

The practical adaptations concern individual equipment fit, secure handling, comfortable movement, appropriate toys, and the dog's own preferences. Do not assume a bully-type dog requires punishment, will enjoy tug, wants canine friends, or tolerates handling because it is physically strong.

## How much practice and whether to use a clicker

[Demant et al. (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applanim.2011.05.010) shows that more consecutive training is not automatically more efficient for the studied task, but its laboratory Beagles do not establish a universal schedule for pets (R13). Use brief practical sessions with rest and adjust to engagement.

[Gilchrist et al. (2021)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33665026/) found no consistent general advantage for clickers over other reward arrangements on their tasks (R11). [Lazarowski et al. (2025)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/veterinary-science/articles/10.3389/fvets.2025.1538452/full) found marker advantages on a more complex working-dog task (R25). A marker can help timing; a clicker purchase is optional, and a soft word may suit a sound-sensitive dog.

## Enrichment, care, and optional household jobs

The [nosework study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applanim.2018.12.009) supports enrichment as a promising welfare activity, not a proven standalone treatment for anxiety (R12). Short searches, gentle play, and useful tasks can be enjoyable when chosen for the individual dog.

Cooperative-care evidence is mixed: [a short veterinary-fear program](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31591343/) and [a cooperative-care pilot](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.applanim.2022.105615) do not justify promising that a chin rest eliminates clinic fear (R17–R18). Teach voluntary participation, honor withdrawal, and coordinate painful or urgent care with the vet.

Complex retrieval uses ordinary shaping and chaining. The optional refrigerator routine is an original example based on retrieval and targeting guides, rather than a trial-tested anxiety treatment. Use a suitable prop and protect against chewing or food access. [ASPCA toxicology guidance](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/aspca-poison-control/people-foods-avoid-feeding-your-pets) establishes why alcohol ingestion must be prevented (P25).

## What the workbook's advancement rules mean

Rules such as four successes in five opportunities across two short sessions are practical criteria chosen for clarity. They are not validated diagnostic cutoffs. Fear-related advancement also depends on comfort, recovery, context, and the ability to leave. A progress chart measures stages marked complete, not a clinical percentage reduction in anxiety. No fictitious training results will be preloaded.
