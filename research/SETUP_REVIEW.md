## NAME — Name recognition

Attention to a name is taught before distraction; a name is not assumed to be a recall or evidence of emotional comfort.

- **NAME-1:** Have tiny rewards within reach in a familiar room. Sit or stand near your relaxed dog, with room for them to move away; no particular posture is needed.
- **NAME-2:** Use the same quiet room. Keep rewards out of sight near your body and begin with your dog nearby, comfortably facing a little away from you.

## MARK — Reward marker

Pair the chosen gentle signal with prompt reward; markers are optional and no later action is required after a terminal marker.

- **MARK-1:** Choose a soft spoken word or test the clicker quietly away from your dog first. Put small rewards within easy reach, with your dog comfortably nearby and your reward hand empty.

## TOUCH — Hand target

Voluntary empty-hand nose targeting transfers only after the basic contact; do not push a hand into the face.

- **TOUCH-1:** Sit or stand beside your dog at a comfortable height. Keep one palm empty and rewards in the other hand; leave clear space beside their nose and an open route away.
- **TOUCH-2:** Start in the familiar hand-target area on a non-slip floor. Keep your target palm empty, rewards hidden in the other hand, and space for one easy step either side.

## SIT — Sit

Comfortable voluntary sitting and early lure fading are appropriate; sitting is not mandatory for safety or proof of calm.

- **SIT-1:** Choose a non-slip area with enough room behind your dog. Have small rewards ready at nose height. If sitting looks uncomfortable, choose a comfortable standing exercise instead.
- **SIT-2:** Use the same non-slip area. Keep the guiding hand empty and rewards in your other hand or pouch, with your dog close enough to see the familiar signal.

## DOWN — Lie down

Reward comfortable voluntary lowering without restraint; posture is distinct from relaxation.

- **DOWN-1:** Provide a soft, non-slip resting surface and space to stretch out. Sit beside your dog with rewards accessible near floor level; use a comfortable stand or sit as the starting position.
- **DOWN-2:** Use the familiar soft surface and leave space for your dog to stand up between turns. Keep the signaling hand empty and rewards in the other hand or pouch.

## STAND — Stand

A comfortable stationary stand is useful independently of a down; avoid repeatedly luring the dog to walk forward.

- **STAND-1:** Use a level, non-slip floor with room in front of your dog. Begin beside a freely offered comfortable sit, with a small reward ready at their natural standing nose height.

## FREE — Release from a position

A release can be taught from any comfortable position; a completed sit is an unnecessary prerequisite.

- **FREE-1:** Choose a familiar room with non-slip footing and a clear patch of floor for a small tossed reward. Have several rewards ready; any comfortable position your dog already offers is suitable.
- **FREE-2:** Use the same safe floor area. Have rewards accessible both for delivery at your dog's position and for a small toss away, with an unobstructed route to move off.

## DROP — Drop an item

Trade willingly without chasing or prying; prevention and guarding assessment are distinct from a generic exchange exercise.

- **DROP-1:** Choose an ordinary dog-safe toy your dog does not guard, with floor space to one side for treats. Have better food rewards ready and keep other dogs away. Skip this setup if item handling causes tension.
- **DROP-2:** Use the same low-value toy and clear trading area, with the better rewards initially out of sight. Avoid treasured or stolen items; a dog with guarding concerns needs an individual plan.

## LEAVE — Leave an item alone

Retain the cue, separately reinforce offered disengagement, teach modest context transfer, and keep hazards inaccessible without supervision.

- **LEAVE-1:** Secure a boring, harmless item behind a barrier or sturdy transparent cover that cannot be knocked aside. Begin far enough away for easy disengagement, with better rewards beside you and away from the item.
- **LEAVE-2:** Prepare one harmless practice item under a sturdy clear cover or behind a secure barrier. Keep the floor clear, better rewards with you and enough distance for an easy turn; protection stays in place.
- **LEAVE-3:** Prepare a familiar protected harmless item and one similarly boring alternative. Keep both inaccessible and present only one at a time; set rewards away from the item and leave room to retreat.
- **LEAVE-4:** Use the familiar secured harmless item in a clear room with better rewards ready. Choose a tiny increase in your distance that still permits active supervision; nobody leaves the room.

## WAIT — Short wait at an indoor doorway

A short safe interior-door pause can be offered or cued; release and physical exit security remain explicit.

- **WAIT-1:** Choose an open interior doorway with no access to outdoors. Clear the floor on both sides, keep the door still, and stand beside your dog with rewards within reach.
- **WAIT-2:** Use the familiar interior doorway, clear of paws and tails throughout the door's swing. Stand with your dog safely back from the edge and keep rewards available; no exterior exit should be exposed.

## TOILET — Toilet routine

Supervision, frequent individual opportunities and rewarding location are appropriate; medical changes are not disobedience.

- **TOILET-1:** Choose an accessible toilet spot and clear the route there. Keep outdoor clothing, a lead if needed, and rewards near the exit so trips after sleep, meals or play can be prompt.
- **TOILET-2:** Keep the familiar toilet route immediately accessible and rewards ready at the toilet spot. Choose the same cue in advance; any request signal must leave access to the door unobstructed.

## RECALL — Come when called, first at home

Teach a distinct inviting approach directly, then transfer gradually; do not assume reliability around hazards or punish arrival.

- **RECALL-1:** Use a secure room with a clear, non-slip path between you and your dog. Have attractive rewards out of sight and room to take one step backward without bumping anything.
- **RECALL-2:** Keep the same enclosed space and clear a slightly longer approach. Have several small rewards accessible at your side. Only include harness contact if your dog already welcomes that separate handling exercise.

## OTARGET — Nose target an object

Teach broad stationary nose contact before placement or motion; this is a reusable contact action, not emotional treatment.

- **OTARGET-1:** Prepare a large, smooth target pad on a secure handle without sharp ends or loose parts. Sit or stand beside your dog, with rewards in the other hand and room beside their face.
- **OTARGET-2:** Use the familiar target and non-slip footing. Leave a few inches of clear space around its usual position at natural head height, and keep rewards separate from the target hand.

## STAY — Stay until released

Increase short duration, distance and distraction separately; release and comfort matter more than a posture quota.

- **STAY-1:** Choose a quiet enclosed room and comfortable non-slip surface. Have rewards ready for delivery at your dog's position and enough clear floor for an easy release; use any comfortable posture.
- **STAY-2:** Use the same surface with clear space behind your own feet for one small step. Keep rewards with you and the dog free to leave; remove nearby distractions before adding handler movement.
- **STAY-3:** Prepare a quiet enclosed area with clear footing for a slight turn or two steps away. Decide beforehand whether this turn changes time or movement, and keep rewards readily accessible.

## STOP — Stop and stand on cue

Reward a modest stationary pause in protected settings; this does not authorize traffic or off-leash safety tests.

- **STOP-1:** Use a secure, level, non-slip space away from traffic and wildlife. Begin beside your dog with room for a few slow steps and rewards at natural standing nose height.
- **STOP-2:** Use the same enclosed area with one or two clear steps between you and your dog. Keep rewards accessible so you can walk to their stopping place without calling them closer.
- **STOP-3:** Choose a secure area with an unobstructed, level walking surface. Start only a few steps from your dog, with rewards ready; this exercise needs slow strolling, not running or real hazards.

## FRONT — Come to a front position

A comfortable arrival position is optional and distinct from a safety recall; do not crowd or physically align the dog.

- **FRONT-1:** Stand on non-slip flooring with an open approach in front of you. Leave about a comfortable dog's length of space before your knees and have small rewards available at their natural head height.
- **FRONT-2:** Use the familiar approach area, with your signal hand empty and rewards elsewhere. Clear space at the usual starting spot and one nearby alternative; a comfortable stand is sufficient.

## FOODWAIT — Wait for a food-bowl invitation

Keep bowl placement very brief and optional; never make meals conditional, repeatedly remove bowls, or treat guarding through compliance.

- **FOODWAIT-1:** Use the usual quiet feeding area with other animals separated. Put part of the normal meal in an easy-to-handle bowl and clear its landing spot. Skip waiting practice if bowl handling causes tension.
- **FOODWAIT-2:** Prepare the familiar bowl and clear feeding spot, with a separate small reward accessible. Stand at a comfortable lowering height with your dog free nearby; the meal remains available without a performance requirement.

## MAT — Go to a mat

Teach voluntary stationing and release separately from relaxed rest; a mat is not an enforced place to endure fear.

- **MAT-1:** Lay a comfortable, flat mat on a non-slip surface away from household traffic. Leave open floor around it for approaches and resets, with small rewards available beside you.
- **MAT-2:** Place the familiar mat in its usual quiet spot, with clear floor one step away. Keep rewards with you rather than scattered on the mat before the cue.

## SETTLE — Relax voluntarily on the mat

Capture comfortable rest independently of cued stationing; a down, food-taking or immobility alone does not establish relaxation.

- **SETTLE-1:** After ordinary toilet, movement and water needs are met, place a comfortable mat near your quiet activity. Keep low-excitement rewards within reach and an open route off the mat.
- **SETTLE-2:** Use the familiar resting spot when your dog is already comfortable there. Minimize nearby activity, have quiet rewards ready, and leave their preferred way to get up unobstructed.
- **SETTLE-3:** Choose a familiar calm resting period and sit near the usual mat or bed. Keep the room uneventful and allow your dog to choose their posture and leave freely.

## SAFE — Choose a safe resting area

A refuge requires actual comfort, escape management and trusted support; confinement or isolation is not automatically soothing.

- **SAFE-1:** Arrange a quiet bed and water away from the entry and busy windows. Leave the route in and out open; use an open crate only if that space is already comfortable.
- **SAFE-2:** Prepare the familiar resting area without visitors present. Have a trusted person comfortably visible and a gate, door or curtains ready for only the small adjustment your dog already tolerates.

## ALONE — Very brief comfortable alone time

Camera-guided below-distress movement, tiny absences and departure-action comfort are distinct components; no crying-out or duration guarantee.

- **ALONE-1:** Place a camera where you can see your dog's whole body in their familiar safe area. Start while they are comfortably resting or occupied, with the floor around your chair clear. Do not test known separation panic.
- **ALONE-2:** Check that the camera shows your dog while you are briefly beyond a nearby interior doorway. Keep the return route clear, the door open and the starting distance within their established comfort.
- **ALONE-3:** Keep your dog in their comfortable familiar area with a camera available to observe their whole body. Choose one quiet departure action and suitable rewards; this exercise includes no departure.

## RESTHEAD — Rest the head on a cushion

Voluntary cushion contact can be taught directly; do not press the head down or infer calm solely from the trained posture.

- **RESTHEAD-1:** Place a stable soft cushion at your dog's natural chin height on a non-slip base. Sit beside it with rewards accessible and a clear route away; avoid anything that tips under light contact.
- **RESTHEAD-2:** Keep the familiar cushion at the same comfortable height. Sit nearby with quiet rewards and clear space for your dog to lift their head and leave without obstruction.

## CRATE — Rest comfortably in a closed crate

Introduce an optional crate with voluntary entry and gradual comfortable closure; it is unsuitable as compulsory panic management.

- **CRATE-1:** Check that the crate allows comfortable standing, turning and lying. Secure the door fully open, add familiar non-slip bedding, and clear the entrance and exit. Remain nearby with rewards.
- **CRATE-2:** Prepare the familiar roomy crate with the door initially secured open. Keep its swing clear of paws and tails, place rewards within reach, and sit beside rather than across the exit.
- **CRATE-3:** Check the familiar crate's latch opens smoothly and rewards can be delivered safely without trapping fingers. Stay beside the door with its exit clear; do not use this setup for panic or unattended absences.

## VACATE — Get off furniture on cue

Reward moving to an inviting alternative without crowding, intimidation or grabbing; existing guarding requires an individual plan.

- **VACATE-1:** Use a low, stable surface only while your dog is awake and comfortable. Clear a non-slip landing or familiar ramp, with rewards accessible from the side; skip practice if approaches cause guarding or pain.
- **VACATE-2:** Keep the same secure landing or ramp and a comfortable alternative resting place nearby. Have the signaling hand empty and rewards ready on the floor side, with your dog awake and unpressured.

## ALLDONE — Finish an activity and move on

Pair a predictable end signal with a comfortable transition; it is not punishment or enforced loss of needed interaction.

- **ALLDONE-1:** Choose a brief familiar game, a safe place to put its prop, and a ready alternative such as water, a sniff area or a bed. Arrange a voluntary trade separately if the dog may hold the prop.
- **ALLDONE-2:** Select a different easy activity and prepare the same familiar quiet alternative. Clear the route between them and a safe place for equipment, with ordinary movement, water and rest needs already met.

## CHECK — Voluntary check-in

Reinforce offered check-ins and gradual transfer; do not demand prolonged eye contact or make food pairing contingent on it.

- **CHECK-1:** Choose a familiar enclosed space with something safe to sniff and room beside your body for rewards. Stand comfortably without blocking your dog, with food readily accessible and no attention cue needed.
- **CHECK-2:** Prepare a clear, quiet walking strip with safe sniffing space nearby. Use comfortable familiar walking equipment if needed, keep the lead slack, and have rewards accessible at your side.

## LOOSE — Loose-leash walking

Teach slack-lead walking on comfortable equipment in small increments; equipment fit is a safety check rather than an obedience grade.

- **LOOSE-1:** Check that the familiar harness or collar fits comfortably and your dog willingly wears it. Use an ordinary lead in a quiet spacious area, with rewards accessible beside your chosen leg.
- **LOOSE-2:** Choose a short, clear stretch on the familiar quiet route. Check comfortable equipment and slack lead handling, with rewards easy to reach before taking the first walking steps.
- **LOOSE-3:** Choose a safe, modestly interesting sniffing patch beside a quiet route. Check the ground and other animals, use comfortable equipment, and leave room to approach without tightening the lead.

## TURN — Happy turn-away

Rehearse an inviting turn before mild distraction, without leash corrections; a retreat remains valid regardless of cue response.

- **TURN-1:** Clear a wide turning arc in a quiet room or secure yard with no trigger present. Stand near your dog with rewards ready and stable footing for two easy steps away.
- **TURN-2:** Pick a quiet outdoor rehearsal area and a safe destination a few steps away. Use familiar comfortable walking equipment and hold the slack lead clear of both your feet.
- **TURN-3:** Choose a familiar quiet route and an easy sniffing spot well away from dogs or other triggers. Identify a clear, rewarding exit direction before starting.

## FIND — Find food on the ground

Invited food search on known safe ground is compatible with leave-it training; avoid competition, ingestion hazards or overwhelming triggers.

- **FIND-1:** Inspect a small clean floor patch and separate other animals. Have a few tiny suitable treats ready, with your dog comfortably nearby and no traffic or food competition.
- **FIND-2:** Check a quiet yard or familiar outdoor patch for litter, unsafe plants and approaching animals. Keep other dogs away and prepare easily visible food pieces off the walking path.

## SIDE — Move behind you or to the far side

Reward a comfortable side position without physical placement; this optional alignment does not replace free movement and sniffing.

- **SIDE-1:** Stand in a quiet room with enough non-slip floor for a wide arc around your legs. Keep the target hand empty, rewards separate, and any lead clear of the route.
- **SIDE-2:** Prepare the same wide side-change space, with room for one forward step after arrival. Keep rewards accessible and avoid real approaching activity during this cue practice.

## HEEL — Heel on cue

Short precise positioning is optional, with early lure fading and releases; it is not the standard for an entire ordinary walk.

- **HEEL-1:** Choose your left or right side in advance. Stand in an enclosed non-slip room with a broad approach arc beside that leg, and have rewards ready at natural nose height.
- **HEEL-2:** Use the same side and clear entry area. Keep the guiding hand empty and rewards in the other hand, with starting space just ahead and slightly to the side.
- **HEEL-3:** Clear a short, straight walking strip in the familiar enclosed room. Use your established side and rewards accessible beside that leg; leave enough space for a relaxed release.
- **HEEL-4:** Prepare a broad turning area with non-slip footing and space for ordinary walking afterward. For outdoor transfer, choose a quiet secure spot and check familiar comfortable equipment before starting.

## RHYTHM — Counted walking rhythm

Predictable short walking patterns can help communication; no claim that a pattern cures fear or overrides the need for distance.

- **RHYTHM-1:** Stand in a quiet safe area with your dog freely nearby. Prepare suitable small rewards for delivery beside you, keeping your hands neutral and an open route for the dog to opt out.
- **RHYTHM-2:** Choose a short, flat walking strip in the familiar quiet area. Have rewards easy to reach beside you, comfortable equipment if needed, and space for a break away from distractions.

## CURB — Pause at a curb

Start at harmless flat lines, then a protected real curb with human traffic decisions; no automatic road-safety claim.

- **CURB-1:** Lay a flat contrasting strip on a non-slip floor, with several clear steps on each side. Avoid raised edges or obstacles and have rewards ready beside your dog.
- **CURB-2:** Choose a protected curb with generous standing space well away from moving vehicles. Check secure comfortable equipment and a clear retreat; an adult remains responsible for every crossing decision.

## CROSS — Cross a quiet road together

Indoor rehearsal follows flat-line preparation; the actual curb prerequisite moves to the real crossing, where humans decide safety.

- **CROSS-1:** Mark two harmless flat lines a few steps apart on a non-slip indoor floor. Clear the route between them, prepare rewards beyond the far line, and use a comfortable familiar lead setup.
- **CROSS-2:** Choose a quiet lawful crossing with clear sight lines and safe standing space beyond both curbs. A capable adult checks secure equipment and traffic; have rewards accessible for the far side.

## SLOW — Match a slower walking pace

Reward modest changes to walking pace without force; exercise and pace must suit individual health and normal movement.

- **SLOW-1:** Choose a short, flat, unobstructed part of a familiar quiet route. Check comfortable walking equipment and have rewards at your side; begin at your dog's ordinary comfortable pace.
- **SLOW-2:** Use the successful flat route with clear space for alternating normal and slightly slower stretches. Keep rewards accessible; prepare a second equally quiet route only after the familiar one is easy.

## WALKPAUSE — Pause while the handler stops

Practice brief comfortable pauses and release rather than prolonged suppression of exploration or distress.

- **WALKPAUSE-1:** Choose a protected quiet walking strip and a roomy stopping spot. Check familiar equipment, ordinary toilet and movement needs, and rewards accessible without shortening the lead.
- **WALKPAUSE-2:** Use the familiar spacious stopping spot. Have rewards ready and choose one harmless distraction such as an empty pocket or imaginary sign; no tether or busy waiting area is needed.

## DOG — Seeing a dog predicts good things

Brief supported sightings and prompt trigger-food pairing begin at a comfortable distance; no obedience or threshold testing is required.

- **DOG-1:** Agree with a capable helper on a calm adult dog, wide separation and a way to end the view. Check comfortable secure equipment and a retreat that does not pass the helper; start conservatively far away.
- **DOG-2:** Use the recorded comfortable separation with the same helper and dog. Arrange brief views that the helper can end, keep desired food ready with you, and leave the retreat completely clear.
- **DOG-3:** Keep the successful distance, secure equipment and retreat unchanged. Agree that the helper will make only one small sideways movement on request; prepare rewards and an immediate way to end the view.

## DISENGAGE — Choose to look away from another dog

Teach an offered attention shift at an easy distance, distinct from counterconditioning; never wait through escalating fixation.

- **DISENGAGE-1:** Recreate the successful DOG viewing distance with the same calm helper dog, secure equipment and clear retreat. Keep rewards with you and the helper ready to end the view before attention becomes fixed.
- **DISENGAGE-2:** Use the established comfortable viewing arrangement and mark out a few clear steps farther away. Coordinate breaks with the helper and keep rewards accessible without tightening the lead.

## PARALLEL — Walk parallel at a comfortable distance

Both dogs need comfortable wide movement and retreat; trigger-food support need not wait for a check-in or eventual greeting.

- **PARALLEL-1:** Arrange two capable handlers on wide, separate walking lines in a quiet open area. Start at the established comfortable separation, check equipment, and agree on the same direction and outward break routes.
- **PARALLEL-2:** Use the same successful separation, direction and calm helper dog. Choose a slightly longer clear stretch with separate sniff-break areas and ample room to widen apart without crossing paths.

## PASS — Make a wide planned pass

The first stationary-helper pass needs dog comfort and a turn; moving-helper competence is introduced only for a moving pass.

- **PASS-1:** Place a capable helper and calm stationary dog well away from a broad curved walking line. Check comfortable secure equipment, rewards and a clear exit beyond the arc; avoid narrow paths.
- **PASS-2:** Keep the successful wide arc and separate distant helper line. Agree on slow opposite-direction movement and an early-stop signal, with clear exits and rewards available to both handlers.

## STRANGER — Feel safer with an unfamiliar adult

Use a distant neutral adult with optional retreat, no food lure toward touch and no obligatory greeting.

- **STRANGER-1:** Arrange a cooperative adult sideways at a generous distance in a quiet open area. Agree that they will ignore your dog without approaching or offering food; keep your own rewards and retreat ready.
- **STRANGER-2:** Recreate the comfortable adult-helper distance and retreat. Agree on one tiny hand movement and a stop signal, with desired rewards beside you; keep all other helper behavior unchanged.
- **STRANGER-3:** Arrange a different cooperative adult, beginning again with the stationary sideways posture and original easy distance. Keep secure equipment, your own rewards and an open retreat; no greeting or touch is planned.

## KNOCK — Feel comfortable hearing a knock

Separate a faint controllable tap from mat behavior and the real arrival chain; do not repeat overwhelming knocks waiting for quiet.

- **KNOCK-1:** Prevent surprise arrivals and choose a quiet time well away from the entrance. Prepare a soft tapping surface and desired rewards; place any helper farther away with a clear stop signal.
- **KNOCK-2:** Arrange a helper outside the closed entry door with no visitor entering. Keep your dog at the previously comfortable indoor distance, rewards beside you, and an agreed signal for one very soft tap.
- **KNOCK-3:** Keep the successful closed-door arrangement and a helper who can stop immediately. Agree beforehand on either an extra faint tap or a slightly stronger single tap, with rewards and quiet breaks ready.

## BELL — Feel comfortable hearing the doorbell

Faint recording and physical bell are separate contexts; pair the noticed sound without requiring silence or a station first.

- **BELL-1:** Prepare a brief bell recording and a speaker you can stop immediately. Set a faint volume with the speaker away from your dog's resting area, prevent actual arrivals, and have desired rewards ready.
- **BELL-2:** Arrange a helper at the actual bell without anyone entering. Keep your dog far inside with rewards ready and the door closed; use a recording instead if the real bell cannot be made sufficiently gentle.

## DOOR — Knock means go to the mat

Combine fluent arrival components under controlled rehearsal; an entry-management plan remains necessary for unplanned arrivals.

- **DOOR-1:** Place the familiar mat at an easy distance from the closed entry door. Use only the previously comfortable knock, with rewards ready at the mat and no visitors or unexpected arrivals planned.
- **DOOR-2:** Keep the successful mat location and gentle knock source unchanged. Have rewards ready for delivery on the mat and enough quiet time for short pauses; no visitor or extra door movement is included.
- **DOOR-3:** Secure a separate barrier before any entry opening. Place the mat at its successful distance, clear the handler's route to the door, and prepare rewards; nobody will enter during practice.

## VISITOR — Comfortable separated visitor routine

A fully separated comfortable visit is a valid endpoint; optional distant viewing must not block nonvisual visitor-action practice.

- **VISITOR-1:** Prepare the dog's already comfortable refuge, a secure barrier and a suitable familiar activity if enjoyed. Arrange trusted company if needed and clear your route to the entry; no visitor is present.
- **VISITOR-2:** Arrange one cooperative adult for a no-knock arrival and a quiet seat away from the refuge. Secure your dog in the familiar comfortable arrangement first, with monitoring or trusted company and rewards ready.
- **VISITOR-3:** Only use an established safe separated arrangement and comfortable STRANGER starting distance. Seat the adult behind a secure barrier, keep your own rewards ready, and ensure you can end the view immediately.
- **VISITOR-4:** Recreate the successful separated or viewing arrangement with the same adult and secure barrier. Agree on one quiet word or small movement and a stop signal, with your rewards and retreat ready.

## GREET — Four paws greeting with familiar people

Greeting is voluntary and unnecessary for successful coexistence; approach for food is not permission to touch.

- **GREET-1:** Arrange one familiar adult your dog already enjoys, beginning at an easy distance. Clear a non-slip approach and retreat, have rewards ready low beside you, and agree on one slow approach step.
- **GREET-2:** Use the familiar adult and successful roomy approach area. Agree that contact is optional and brief, with pauses and an unobstructed exit; keep rewards available without drawing the dog into touch.

## HANDOFF — Change leash handlers comfortably

Use a familiar consenting helper and reversible small changes; transferred equipment does not justify restraint or separation distress.

- **HANDOFF-1:** Choose a secure enclosed walking area and two familiar capable adults. Check comfortable fitted equipment; the usual handler keeps the lead while the helper has rewards and space to walk nearby.
- **HANDOFF-2:** Pause in the enclosed area with the usual handler securely holding the lead. Identify a safe hand-to-hand grip away from your dog's head and remove tangles; have rewards accessible without releasing control.
- **HANDOFF-3:** Recreate the successful secure transfer arrangement, with a short clear walking line and the first adult staying close. Check equipment and rewards before the familiar helper takes control; no departure is planned.

## SOUND — Controlled sound practice

Controlled brief sound-food pairing changes one dimension at a time; replace historical no-comfort guidance and avoid recording-to-real-event guarantees.

- **SOUND-1:** Choose one brief sound clip in a quiet home and a player with an immediate stop control. Set a faint volume and distant speaker position, note both settings, and keep desired food ready.
- **SOUND-2:** Recreate the successful recording, speaker position and volume. Decide on one tiny duration or volume change before starting, with an immediate stop control and rewards within reach.
- **SOUND-3:** Choose one modest source or location variation and reset playback to an easy quiet level. Keep the dog's familiar retreat open, rewards ready, and real frightening noise events outside this practice.

## OUTSIDE — Noises outside and window activity

Different mild outdoor sights and sounds need separately controlled starts; visual and auditory presentations are not interchangeable certification.

- **OUTSIDE-1:** Prepare a comfortable resting area away from busy windows, with view screening and an open retreat. Check any gentle masking sound separately and have rewards ready while nothing is happening outside.
- **OUTSIDE-2:** Use the screened comfortable area and agree on one faint scheduled event with a helper far outside. Keep food beside you, a clear stop signal and the option to end exposure immediately.
- **OUTSIDE-3:** Keep the established low-level event and screening arrangement. Clear a short route away from the window to the familiar rest area, with rewards ready there and no obstacle to retreat.

## STORM — Prepare for unavoidable noise events

Rehearse protection and support before real events; plan readiness can be recorded without inducing noise or claiming fear is cured.

- **STORM-1:** Choose a quiet day to prepare familiar bedding, curtains and secure exits in the refuge. Have water and trusted company available; use masking sound only if already comfortable and follow any veterinary plan as prescribed.
- **STORM-2:** On a quiet day, have your refuge checklist, support contacts and any existing veterinary instructions ready. Rehearse ordinary preparations without noise exposure; no storm or fireworks event is needed.

## OBJECT — Comfort with an object or appliance

Still sight, motion and sound can be conditioned separately; food does not require exploration, and combinations await comfortable components.

- **OBJECT-1:** Place the object switched off and stable at a generous distance. Keep your dog's route away clear without crossing its path, have rewards with you, and screen part of the view if needed.
- **OBJECT-2:** Keep the object switched off at its established comfortable distance. Arrange a helper for one tiny movement and an immediate stop, with your dog's exit clear and food ready beside you.
- **OBJECT-3:** Prepare a faint recording or a helper controlling the appliance behind a distant closed door. Keep the dog in a comfortable separate area with rewards and an immediate sound cutoff; do not add visible motion yet.

## MOTION — Bikes, moving vehicles, or joggers at a distance

Start well away from traffic or controlled moving helpers with retreat; use physical security and avoid motion-based flooding.

- **MOTION-1:** Choose one trigger type and a protected stationary viewing spot with a clear retreat. Arrange a cooperative adult far away for a slow controlled demonstration, check secure equipment, and keep rewards ready.
- **MOTION-2:** Keep the successful protected location and wide separation. Agree with the helper on either a very small speed change or a duration change, with a stop signal, secure equipment and rewards available.

## CAR — Comfort around a parked car

Stationary-car comfort precedes travel; use appropriate transport restraint and veterinary assessment of motion sickness or severe distress.

- **CAR-1:** Park in a safe quiet place, switch the engine off and check a comfortable temperature. Secure doors against unexpected movement, choose an open retreat, and have rewards ready at a comfortable viewing distance.
- **CAR-2:** Keep the parked engine-off car cool and the door secured open. Prepare stable non-slip footing or a suitable ramp and a clear immediate exit, with rewards accessible both inside and outside.
- **CAR-3:** Use the familiar travel restraint in the comfortable parked car with a trusted adult beside the dog. Keep doors under human control and the exit clear; engine noise and driving are not part of this setup.

## ELEVATOR — Ride a quiet elevator comfortably

Introduce empty-car sounds, voluntary entry and a short ride separately; keep dog and handler together with no leash through closing doors.

- **ELEVATOR-1:** Choose a quiet authorized elevator and a roomy viewing position well outside its doorway. Arrange a helper, secure comfortable equipment, rewards and an unobstructed retreat; avoid passenger traffic.
- **ELEVATOR-2:** Arrange a helper to operate door controls according to building rules. Use an empty stationary car with non-slip footing, enough turning room and lead slack; keep all feet and equipment clear of the door tracks.
- **ELEVATOR-3:** Plan one short quiet ride after comfortable entries. Have a familiar adult beside the dog, rewards ready and a clear exit at the destination; avoid crowds and keep the lead away from door tracks.

## AUTODOOR — Pass through automatic doors

Observe safely before a controlled broad passage; never rely on a sensor detecting a dog or put the dog in the opening to activate it.

- **AUTODOOR-1:** Choose a quiet permitted entrance with a cooperative helper. Stand to one side outside the sensor area, with secure equipment, rewards and a broad retreat; the helper will activate the panels.
- **AUTODOOR-2:** Arrange the helper to activate the doors and verify a fully open, generous passage. Clear the approach and far-side reward area, check lead and tail clearance, and retain room to turn away before entry.

## HARNESS — Comfortable harness routine

Voluntary approach, head passage and comfortable fitting are separate; fit, movement and withdrawal matter more than a completed grade.

- **HARNESS-1:** Have the harness unfastened in a quiet room, with rewards separate and room for your dog to move away. Check the buckle sound away from them and begin at a comfortable viewing distance.
